Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to common questions about AI, online safety, and tech careers today.

Will AI take my job?

For most people, AI is more likely to change a job than erase it. The World Economic Forum expects 170 million new jobs and 92 million displaced jobs worldwide by 2030, a net gain of 78 million, but nearly 40% of today's job skills are expected to change. The safest move is learning to use AI well in your field. Source ↗

How can I tell if an email or text is a scam?

Watch for urgency ("act now or your account closes"), a sender address that doesn't match the company, links that go somewhere unexpected, and requests for passwords, gift cards, or crypto. When in doubt, don't click. Go to the company's website or app yourself. Scams are costly: Americans reported $20.9 billion in cybercrime losses to the FBI in 2025. Source ↗

What is multi-factor authentication, and should I use it?

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) asks for a second proof of identity, like a code from an app, on top of your password. Yes, turn it on everywhere you can, starting with email and banking. Microsoft has found accounts are more than 99.9% less likely to be compromised when MFA is enabled. Source ↗

My information was in a data breach. What should I do?

Change the password for that account and anywhere you reused it, turn on MFA, and watch your bank and credit card statements. In the U.S. you can also freeze your credit for free with Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, which stops anyone from opening new accounts in your name. Breaches are common and expensive: the global average cost reached $4.99 million in 2026. Source ↗

Is it safe to share personal information with AI chatbots?

Treat a chatbot like any online service. Avoid sharing passwords, Social Security numbers, financial details, or private medical information, and check the privacy settings to see whether your chats are stored or used for training. Remember that AI can also be confidently wrong, so double-check important answers.

Is cybersecurity a good career right now?

Yes. Information security analyst jobs are projected to grow 21% from 2025 to 2035, much faster than average, with a median salary of $129,180. Demand stays high because 95% of organizations report at least one skills gap on their security teams. Source ↗

Is a computer science degree still worth it?

A CS degree still leads to strong long-term careers. Software developers earn a median of $135,980 and the field is projected to grow 10% by 2035. The challenge is the first job: recent graduates face higher unemployment (about 5.6% in mid-2026) than the overall workforce, so projects, internships, and certifications matter more than ever. Source ↗

How do I start a tech career with no experience?

Start with roles that train you on the job, like IT help desk, technical support, or junior developer positions. Build a few real projects and put them on GitHub, earn an entry-level certification such as CompTIA A+ or Security+, and connect with people in the field on LinkedIn and at local meetups. The Jobs journal covers this in more detail.
